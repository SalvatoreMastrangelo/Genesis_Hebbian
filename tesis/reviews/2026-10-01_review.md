### A1. The fitness of the rules favours flying below the commanded speed, and the slow-and-cheap arrival point of 6.1 is its optimum [claim + decide] (E2; the examiner's third defence question; the text half-states it at ch6:L57)

- **DECLINED 2026-10-01: the author's decision ("ignore"). Keep the derivation as a prepared answer for the defence.**

- Text. ch5:L142 "A flight that holds the commanded speed for $T$ seconds collects $25\,T$"; ch5:L448 the fitness is "the mean ... of the reward summed over the flight"; ch6:L57 "Its tracking term is a bell curve that is flat near the commanded speed, so a flight a few per cent slower costs almost nothing per step, while the flight lasts longer over the same distance"; ch6:L116 "the flight that the fitness of \autoref{subsec:inner-loop} rewards on this body"; ch6:L118 "\emph{Where a base controller ends up under the rules does not depend on where it started.}"; ch7:L19 "A specialist with rules is, in effect, a controller whose flight dynamics are plastic" and "The gain is real in the currency of the search".
- The examiner's derivation, checked. Over a course of fixed length $L$ flown at speed $v$, the tracking term is collected for $25\,L/v$ steps, each worth $\exp(-\tfrac12((v/v^{\star}-1)/0.25)^2)$. With $u = v/v^{\star}$ the sum is proportional to $f(u) = u^{-1}\exp(-\tfrac12((u-1)/0.25)^2)$, and $\mathrm{d}\ln f/\mathrm{d}u = 0$ gives $u^2 - u + 0.0625 = 0$, that is $u = 0.933$: the summed tracking term alone is maximized by flying 7 % below the command, on any body, before the energy term adds its own preference for a slow flight. All four base controllers end at 13.3 to 13.4 m/s under their rules (`tab:inner-loop-bases`), from 13.9. On the search course the specialists complete most flights (124 of 130 m), so the "course of fixed length" case applies to them in full.
- What this changes. 6.1.2 already says that the arrival point is "a property of the body and of the fitness rather than of the controller" and that the rules fit the controller to "the flight that the fitness rewards": the reading is honest. What the text does not say is that the fitness has this preference by construction, independently of the body, and chapter 7 then calls the result "plastic flight dynamics" and "real in the currency of the search" without that qualification. The comparison of 6.2 is not touched (both conditions share the fitness and the exam judges by the objectives). Related: A4 of `review_5` (the fitness weighs bodies by flight time; the first sentence was applied at the end of 5.3.2).
- To decide: (a) state the bias where the fitness is defined and keep the reading of 6.1 with one more clause; or (b) leave 5.3.2 as it is and qualify chapter 7 only.
- Proposed for (a), end of "Fitness" in 5.3.2, after the sentence on the bodies that fly for long: *"The sum has a second preference of its own. The tracking term is collected once per step, so over a course of given length a flight a little slower than commanded collects more of it; on its own, that term is largest about 7 % below the command. The energy term pulls in the same direction. A set of rules that slows the flight down is therefore rewarded on every body."* In 6.1.2, ch6:L116, after "the flight that the fitness ... rewards on this body": *"and that it rewards on any body, since a slower flight collects the summed reward for longer (\autoref{subsec:inner-loop})."* In chapter 7, ch7:L19, replace "towards the flight that the fitness rewards on this body" with *"towards the slower flight that the summed reward favours"*, and "The gain is real in the currency of the search" with *"The gain is nevertheless real in the currency of the search"*.
