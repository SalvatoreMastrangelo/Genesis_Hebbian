Draft wording follows the thesis conventions (no em-dashes, Oxford spelling) and is a proposal.

Legend: **[fact]** the text is wrong or inconsistent; **[gap]** something promised or needed is missing; **[claim]** a statement stronger than its support; **[opt]** optional; **[decide]** needs a decision of the author before any wording. "R / A / E" = raised by the reader, the auditor, the examiner. `ch2`, `ch3`, `ch4` = the three chapter sources.

### A1. What ran for 94 hours: `tab:feasibility`, "sixty forests" and the two meanings of "generation" [fact + decide] (R3, A1, E5: all three reviewers)

- **PARTLY APPLIED 2026-09-21 (author chose option (a) after 5.3 was written):** the row of `tab:feasibility` now reads "One body search in which bodies are scored by rollouts alone, with no training inside the loop: 94 h", which is true of the co-design run that was measured; the 3 200, the hypothetical rows and ch3:58 are unchanged. - Text. ch3:48-49: "Bodies assessed by one body search (64 per generation, 50 generations): 3 200" and "One body search with one fixed controller for all bodies, rollouts only: 94 h". ch3:60: "each body is flown on sixty forests before it is ranked". ch4:53: "a single generation of the body search scores 245 760 of them" (also ch4:6, ch4:140).

- The reader is right. `logs/remote/outer_nsga/outer_exam_4_64_64_300_extra_r1/.../reproducibility/config.yaml`: `num_generations: 200` (l. 40), `num_eval_envs: 245760` (l. 43), CMA-ES `population_size: 64` (l. 88), `num_urdfs: 64` (l. 102), `refresh_urdfs_every: 4` (l. 106), `rescore_top_frac: 0.125` (l. 122); `generations/` gen_000 08-26 19:40 → gen_199 08-30 17:15 = 94 h. So the 94 h and the 3 200 bodies (64 × 50 body refreshes) belong to the **co-design** run, in which every body is flown by 64 candidate controllers, and a "generation" of ch3 (a body refresh) is four "generations" of ch4 (245 760 flights each).
- The searches that really use one fixed controller are the morphology-only runs (`outer_morphology_only_exam_extra_r0/r1`: `eta: 0.0`, `num_generations: 88`, `refresh_urdfs_every: 1`, same 245 760 flights per generation): 64 × 88 = **5 632 bodies in 130 to 132 h**.
- "Sixty forests" is the number of flights of one (body, candidate controller) pair. A body is flown 3 840 times in every generation of either run, and it is ranked from the phase-end exam (the best eighth of the controllers, 8 × 480 forests = 3 840 flights per body; the 480 is from the project notes, confirm it).
- The argument of 3.3 survives either way (rollouts only against a training per body), but the row label, the count of flights and the word "generation" have to agree with chapter 4 and with what chapter 5 will say. Options:

### A2. "By construction, not adapted to any one body": conditioning and recurrence [claim + decide] (R1, E4; examiner's defence question 3)
- Text. ch3:62: "A controller paid for once is, by construction, not adapted to any one body, so its adaptation to the body being scored must come from somewhere else". Against it, the thesis's own chapter 2: MetaMorph is "paid once, for the whole design space, at the price of telling the controller which body it has" (ch2:619), and recurrent networks are introduced precisely for hidden quantities (ch2:197). The base controller of this thesis is an LSTM trained across bodies (heading of ch5), and ch4:465 states without a reason that "no sensor tells it which body it is flying".
- Why it matters: this is the step on which the case for plasticity rests. Both reviewers reached the same question independently: *what can plastic output weights do that the recurrent state cannot, and why may the controller not be told its genome?* The project's own literature notes say the same (a frozen recurrent base can absorb much of the adaptation job).
- Facts: `wp1_config.yaml:94-95` `actor_genome_obs: false`, `critic_genome_obs: true`; `THESIS_DECISIONS.md` §5 asks chapter 5 to *state* that the actor never receives the morphology, not to *justify* it.
- Options:
  - **(a) weaken ch3:62 and make in-flight inference the baseline that plasticity must beat** (this is what the morphology-only against co-design comparison of chapter 6 measures). Proposed: *"A controller paid for once is not trained for any one body. Whatever adaptation it has to the body being scored is what it can infer in flight from its own observations, and the search inherits the limits of that inference. Any adaptation beyond it must come from somewhere else, and must cost no more than the rollouts that score the body anyway."* No method is named, so the rule of chapter 3 holds. In 2.5.3 add memory-based adaptation to the enumeration next to conditioning.
  - **(b) leave chapter 3 as it is** and carry the whole answer in chapter 5 (why the body is hidden from the actor; what the rules add to the LSTM). The sentence "by construction" stays exposed until then.
- In both cases section D lists the justification as owed by chapter 5.

### A3. Fine-tuning as the comparator of 3.3, and the status of "about an hour" [claim + decide] (E5, R10c, A19)

The caption of `tab:feasibility` calls the last two rows hypothetical; the hour itself is the author's statement (`THESIS_DECISIONS.md` §6) and the text does not say whether it is measured, nor whether it starts from scratch.

Also say in the prose what the hour is: *"about an hour in our runs"* if measured.

### A4. The 30 N force limit binds for large wings at normal speed [claim + decide] (E3, A10; extends `CH4_CORRECTIONS.md` B4)
- Text. ch4:262: "the force on each element is limited to 30 N, several times the weight of the drone". Code: `genesis/engine/solvers/drones/simple_drone.py:38` (`force_cap: 30.0`), applied per element at l. 1120 and l. 1235.
- B4 already notes that the heaviest body is held to 2.4 g. The examiner adds the stronger case: the largest panel that the genome allows (span 0.9 m, aspect ratio 1.5, 0.54 m²) reaches 30 N at C_L = 0.40 at 15 m/s (q = 138 Pa), far below C_Lmax. For large wings the limit is therefore active in ordinary flight, not only in diverging states, and it can shape one end of the Pareto front. The fixed T_max = 5.2 N has the same character: thrust to weight 0.88 for the reference drone, 0.21 for the heaviest body.
- To do before wording: measure on the front bodies of one production run how often an element sits at the limit (one instrumented exam flight per body is enough). Then either (i) it is rare: say so with the number, and word B4 as proposed there; or (ii) it is not: present the limit as a constraint of the design space in "Limits of the model", and keep it in mind when reading the large-wing end of the fronts.
