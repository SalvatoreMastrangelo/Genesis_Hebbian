## A. Decisions to take first

### A1. DECIDED 2026-09-21: the author keeps the η = 0 control as it is and lists the separating experiment (re-fly with D = 0 and with D alone) in `POSSIBLE_EXPERIMENTS.md`, item 1, as something he may run before finishing the thesis (not future work); the wording of ch5:278 was already replaced when 5.3.5 was written. Through D the rule genome contains a static retuning of the output layer, so "with plasticity against without" does not isolate plasticity [claim + decide] (E3; introduced today)

- ch5:278: "the comparison between a body scored with plasticity and the same body scored without it involves no other difference". ch5:333: "$D_{ij}$ is not plastic at all ... Through $\mat{D}$ the search can retune the output layer itself."
- The two sentences are both true and they collide. By `eq:hebbian-trace` the contribution of $D_{ij}$ is the constant $\eta D_{ij}/\lambda$ (up to 0.15, three times the mean trained weight), the same on every body. CMA-ES therefore also performs a direct search over 224 static output weights on the evaluation fitness, which differs from the PPO objective (undiscounted sum, 1 cm margin, narrower speeds). A gain over the zero-rule baseline can come from there and says nothing about adaptation to a body. Code: `src/WP2/hebbian.py:144-152` (the D term has no activity factor).
- To decide: (a) is there, or will there be, a run that separates the two (rules with D forced to zero, or D only)? (b) if not, how chapter 6 words its gains. The evolved genomes are on disk, so the share of $\eta D/\lambda$ in the realized $\Delta W$ of the champions can at least be reported (`champion_deltaw_diff.py` already reads the $\Delta W$ histories).
- Wording that holds in either case, ch5:278: "...is the natural control condition of the experiments: the two are flown through the same procedure, and they differ only in the coefficients." And at the end of the paragraph on D (ch5:333): "A gain obtained through $\mat{D}$ alone is a gain of the search and not of plasticity; \autoref{ch:results} has to tell the two apart."

### A2. "Adapt to the body" against a memory of 0.8 s [claim + decide] (E10; R1 and E2 touch it; introduced today, flagged by the writer in `THESIS_DECISIONS.md`)

- ch5:220: "to adapt to a body a solution of the task that was learned for all bodies". ch5:329: "The weights therefore describe the last second of the flight and not the whole of it ... closer to a continuous retuning of the gains".
- The examiner reads the second sentence as undercutting the first: with $\lambda = 0.05$ the deviation is one fixed bilinear filter of the last twenty steps of $(\vect{x}, \vect{\mu})$, identical on every body, whose state follows the manoeuvre at least as much as the body; "information about the body reaches the rule through the activity" is true of any feedback controller; the pitch example of ch5:289 is an illustration, not evidence.
- The text is honest about this, which is why the question arises. To decide: keep "adapt to the body" as the claim of the method and make chapter 6 show that the weights of different bodies differ (mean $\Delta W$ per body over the 60 forests would do, the data exist), or use a weaker word ("in-flight modulation of the gains") until then. Also owed: a reason for $\lambda = 0.05$ beyond "set by hand in preliminary runs" (ch5:363); 0.02 was also tried (`src/WP2/experiments`, two configs).

### A3. PARTLY APPLIED 2026-09-21: the author's reason is now at ch5:77 ("The purpose is to let it learn to fly any morphology without hints on what its flight dynamics could be."); ch3:63 "by construction" and ch5:291 "over any fixed weights" were not touched. The genome is withheld "on purpose", and the purpose is nowhere [gap + decide] (R1, A7, E1, E2: all three, and the examiner's first two defence questions; follows A3 of `review_3`, where the author's line was applied)

- ch5:77: "One input is absent on purpose". ch3:63: "A controller paid for once is, by construction, not adapted to any one body". ch2:623, ch2:650: MetaMorph is "paid once" and is adapted through its input. ch5:79: the critic does receive the genome.
- The decision of today (the controller does not know the genome; the LSTM makes it stateful) removed the writer's justification and left none. All three readers ask the same two questions: why not give the actor the genome, which in simulation is free, and why the recurrent state is not counted as adaptation (the examiner adds that a recurrent policy trained over 256 hidden bodies with its own commands as input is the standard set-up of implicit identification, and that `peng2018sim`, cited at ch4:269, makes that point).
- This needs the author's actual reason (deployment without a description of the body? a controller that must fly bodies proposed by a search without retraining its input? a result of an earlier experiment?). One sentence at ch5:77 is enough, and ch3:63 should then say "a controller that is not told the body" instead of "by construction".
- Residue of the same decision at ch5:291: "has something to gain over any fixed weights". The argument of Soltoggio et al. and the result of Najarro and Risi concern memoryless networks; here the comparison is with a recurrent generalist whose output layer is fixed. Proposed: "has something to gain over a fixed output layer".

### A4. "Inner" and "outer" before 5.3 is written [decide] (R3; B5 of `review_2`, sharper now)

- ch2:596: the rule search is the *outer* level and the lifetime the inner one. ch2:606-611 and all of chapter 3: "inner" is the per-body problem $\vect{\theta}^{\star}(\vect{m})$. ch5:367: "Inner Loop: Evolving the Hebbian Rules". ch5:291 (new): "a set of rules is never scored on one body".
- The thesis's inner loop is therefore not the inner problem of `eq:codesign-bilevel`: the rules are shared by all bodies, and the per-body level is the flight itself. A reader coming from chapters 2 and 3 will read 5.3 as "solve $\vect{\theta}^{\star}(\vect{m})$ for each body". Decide the three names (flight or lifetime, rule search, body search) and say once, at the top of 5.3, how they map onto `eq:codesign-bilevel`.

- Proposed: "In the works of \autoref{subsec:rw-plasticity} that evolve their rules, the weights are drawn at random at the start of every episode, and the rules have to build a controller out of nothing every time; where a trained part exists \cite{miconi2018differentiable}, it is trained together with the rule, by the same gradient."

### B5. The analogy with MAML is worded as a finding about this network [claim] (E13; introduced today)

- ch5:285: "the finding says that the last layer is where adapting to a new one pays". Raghu et al. concerns networks that were meta-trained, feed-forward, mostly on few-shot classification, where the head must change because the labels do.
- Proposed: "...the generalist is a network whose features were trained across 256 of them, and the finding suggests, by analogy, that the last layer is where adapting to a new one pays. The analogy is loose, since the generalist was not meta-trained and its tasks share the meaning of their outputs; it motivates the choice and does not prove it."

### B6. "It contains nothing that depends on the body" [claim] (E15; written with 5.1.3, not raised before)

- ch5:120. The smoothness and symmetry terms are in radians, and the joint ranges depend on the gear genes (`eq:drone-gear`): the same normalized change of a command costs up to $(3.5/1.5)^2 = 5.4$ times more on one body than on another. The energy term is in absolute watts. Since the reward is also the fitness of the rules, averaged over bodies, the scaling matters.
- Proposed: "and no parameter of the body appears in it, although two terms scale with the body: the joint angles are in radians, so their ranges follow the gear ratios, and the power is absolute."

### B7. "No measure of its airspeed" in still air [claim] (R12, A10; not in the earlier reports)

- ch4:465 and ch5:61 list the airspeed among the unmeasured quantities; ch4:142: "The air is assumed at rest"; `tab:drone-sensors` gives the velocity in the world frame and the attitude. Without wind the airspeed follows from the two, up to sensor noise, so it is a weak reason for partial observability. The other reasons (rates, joint angles, latency, noise) stand.
- Proposed: drop "its airspeed" at ch5:61, and at ch4:465 write "no direct measure of its airspeed" or drop it.

### B8. The reason given for the noise on the critic's genome [claim + decide] (E ledger; the writer's own reading, flagged in `THESIS_DECISIONS.md` section 5)
