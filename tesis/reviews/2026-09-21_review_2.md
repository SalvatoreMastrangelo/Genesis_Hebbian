## A. Decisions to take first

### A1. `F(m, θ)` is a scalar with a total order, the task has two objectives [gap + decide] (A4)
- Text. ch2:606-609 (`eq:codesign-bilevel`): "$F(\vect{m}, \vect{\theta})$ for the performance of the closed loop", with two arg max. ch3:22-27 (`eq:ranking-flip`): "$F(\vect{m}_1, \vect{\theta}_G) > F(\vect{m}_2, \vect{\theta}_G)$". Against ch3:4 ("judged on how far it gets and on the energy it spends"), ch3:13 ("or dominate on both") and ch4:301 ("the first of the two quantities by which bodies and controllers are judged").
- Why it matters: arg max and ">" are undefined for the pair (progress, cost of transport). The ranking flip is the core of 3.2 and holds only once the text says what is being compared. It also hides a fact of the method that chapter 5 will have to state: the two levels do not optimize the same thing.
- Code: the inner level maximizes a scalar, the sum of the rewards of the episode averaged over the forests (`src/WP2/evaluate.py:8`, `:1034-1042`, `cmaes.fitness_aggregator: mean`); the outer level ranks bodies on `progress_m` and `cost_of_transport` (`src/WP2_Outer_Loop/configs/outer_nsga_default.yaml:68-72`).
- Options:
  - **(a) keep the scalar and say so.** ch3:4, after "that can only be measured by flying them": *"Here $F$ stands for any scalar measure of that performance; with the two objectives of this thesis a body is compared with another by dominance, and the argument of this chapter holds for each objective separately."* No method is named, so the rule of chapter 3 holds. Chapter 5 then states which scalar the controller search maximizes (section D).
  - **(b) make `F` a vector** in 2.5.1 and 3.2 and read ">" as dominance. Heavier, and the inner arg max over $\vect{\theta}$ stays ill-defined, because the inner level of this thesis really is scalar.

### A2. The cost of transport does not count the energy the drone is released with [claim + decide] (E6)
- Text. ch4:455-460: "The energy $E$ of a flight is the integral of the total power up to the end of the flight", CoT = E/(m g Δx); ch4:290: released "15~m above the ground"; release speed not in the text (15 m/s, `env.py:917`, already B7 of `CH4_CORRECTIONS.md`).
- The examiner's arithmetic, checked for the reference drone (0.605 kg): potential energy of 15 m = 89 J, kinetic energy at 15 m/s = 68 J, together 157 J; a flight of 190 m at CoT 0.23 draws 0.23 × 0.605 × 9.81 × 190 = 259 J from the battery. A drone that trades its height and speed for distance could therefore show a CoT well below its sustained value (by up to about 40 \% in this example, if all of it were spent), and the shorter the flight the larger the share: this can bend a progress/CoT front.
- What speaks against it in the code: the reward penalizes the altitude error and the energy (`wp1_config.yaml:118-120`, `energy: -0.001`, `height: -0.003`), and a flight below 0.1 m ends. Nothing in the text says so, and nothing measures it.
- To do before wording: from the exam logs of one production run, the mean altitude and speed at the end of the flights of the front bodies. Then either (i) the change of mechanical energy is small: say it with the number in 4.3.1, e.g. *"The height and the speed of the release are not counted in $E$; the flights of this thesis end on average within … m of the release altitude, so the energy recovered from them is below … \% of $E$."*; or (ii) it is not: correct E by the change of mechanical energy, or state the limit.

### A3. Why evolution and not the gradient: the reason given in 2.5.2 is wrong [fact + decide] (E7)
- Text. ch2:635: "The price of differentiability is that the lifetime must be differentiable: the base weights and the rule are co-adapted by the same gradient, and the method does not apply when the lifetime is a closed-loop interaction with a physics simulator, which is why the works below, and this thesis, return to evolution." The same idea, milder, at ch2:596.
- The gradient of differentiable plasticity runs through the unrolled network, not through the environment: Miconi et al. 2018 include a reinforcement learning task (maze exploration, trained with an actor-critic policy gradient) and Backpropamine is evaluated on reinforcement learning as well. It is how the PPO of this thesis trains an LSTM, and ch2:128 itself says that the policy gradient needs no knowledge of the dynamics. The sentence also omits the maze task from the list of their results.
- Why [decide]: the replacement sentence states why this thesis uses evolution, which is the author's argument to make (chapter 5 will repeat it). The examiner's follow-up is "why not train the ABCD coefficients with PPO on the frozen base?".
- Proposed, to be adapted: *"... on few-shot image classification and on a maze exploration task learned by reinforcement. The price of differentiability is that the gradient has to be carried back through every weight update of the lifetime, which for episodes of thousands of steps is costly in memory and poorly conditioned, and that the base weights and the rule are co-adapted by that same gradient. The works below, and this thesis, use evolution instead, which needs only the outcome of a lifetime and places no condition on how that outcome is computed."* ch2:596 can stay: it says "long, indirect, and only defined by running it", which is true.
