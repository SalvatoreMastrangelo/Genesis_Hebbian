# Thesis writing decisions

Running record of the choices made while writing the thesis in this folder, so that any agent (or the author) picking up a later chapter applies the same conventions and knows what earlier chapters promised, deferred, or deliberately left out. Update this file whenever a new decision is taken. Last update: 2026-09-21.

## 1. Build and check

```bash
cd tesis && latexmk -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode thesis.tex   # biber + pdflatex, all passes
grep -c "^! \|Undefined\|undefined" thesis.log                   # must be 0 (grep only AFTER latexmk has exited)
grep -c "—" chapters/*/*.tex chapters/Bibliografia.bib           # must be 0 everywhere (no em-dashes)
pdftoppm -f N -l N -r 70 -png thesis.pdf /tmp/page && <look at the PNG>   # always look at new pages
```

`thesis.log` line 299 `\end{dedication}` is a pre-existing hyperref duplicate-destination warning, not an error. `\nocite{*}` is commented out: uncited bib entries do not print.

### Whole-thesis review routine (set up 2026-09-21)

After any major change of the corpus (a subsection written or rewritten, many small edits, a term or a number changed throughout) the whole thesis is reviewed, not only the part that changed: the `thesis-review` skill (`/thesis-review`, files in `.claude/skills/thesis-review/`). It runs the mechanical checks, has three fresh-context reviewers read the entire thesis cold (flow of information, internal consistency, sceptical examiner; they never see this file or the code), then verifies every finding against the source, this file and the code, and writes `reviews/YYYY-MM-DD_review.md` in the format of `CH4_CORRECTIONS.md`. **It applies nothing**: fixes are made only on the author's command ("apply B1 B3"), after which decisions are recorded here (§5, §6) and declined findings in §9.

The trigger does not rely on memory: `scripts/review_state.py` keeps a copy of the reviewed sources in `reviews/.snapshot/` and measures the change in words (paragraphs are single source lines, so lines mean nothing). A Stop hook asks for the review when a session has changed at least 400 words, or at least 10 paragraphs with 50 words; a SessionStart hook announces a pending review after edits made by hand. Thresholds are constants at the top of the script; `python3 .claude/skills/thesis-review/scripts/review_state.py status` shows the current count.

## 2. Global conventions

- **Language and spelling.** English (`babel english`). Oxford style as used in chapter 2: `-ize` / `-ization` (optimization, parametrization, normalization) but otherwise British (behaviour, centre, manoeuvrability, labour).
- **No em-dashes anywhere** in thesis prose, captions or bib. Use commas, colons, a parenthesis, or a new sentence.
- **Register.** Flowing prose paragraphs; `\emph{}` on a term at first use; numbered display equations with `\label{eq:...}` referenced by `\eqref{}`; sections, tables and chapters referenced by `\autoref{}` (English names, "section 2.3", "chapter 5"). Numeric citations `\cite{key}`; several keys in one `\cite{}` when citing a group.
- **Notation standard: everything that is not a scalar is bold** (supervisor's request, 2026-09-21; applied to chapters 2, 3 and 4). Vectors are written `\vect{x}`, `\vect{\theta}` and matrices `\mat{W}`; both macros are defined in the preamble of `thesis.tex` as `\bm{#1}` (package `bm`, bold italic for Latin and Greek alike). Never write `\mathbf` or `\bm` directly, so that the style is decided in one place (e.g. `\newcommand{\mat}[1]{#1}` gives plain matrices). **The convention is applied but not stated in the thesis**: a notation paragraph at the start of chapter 2 was written and dropped the same day on the author's decision (2026-09-21); do not add it back. Plain italic: scalars; the single components of a vector or matrix (`x_j`, `W_{ij}`, `q_j`, `w_{i,z}`, `\tau_{\mathrm{act},j}`); the ABCD coefficients when they are discussed as the coefficient of one synapse; **the elements of abstract sets, i.e. the states `s` and actions `a` of the MDP in 2.1** (as in Sutton and Barto), while the concrete seven-command vector of chapter 4 is `\vect{a}`; sets (`\mathcal{S}`, populations `P`, `Q`, fronts `\mathcal{F}_i`); axis names `x`, `y`, `z`. Bold: parameter vectors (`\vect{\theta}`, critic `\vect{w}`, also as subscripts: `\pi_{\vect{\theta}}`, `V_{\vect{w}}`), RNN/LSTM states, gates and biases, CMA-ES `\vect{m}`, `\vect{x}_k`, `\vect{p}_c`, `\mat{C}`, solutions `\vect{x}` and objective vector `\vect{f}` (and the points `\vect{p}`, `\vect{q}_i` of `fig:pareto`), Hebbian `\vect{x}`, `\vect{y}`, `\vect{w}`, `\mat{W}` (but scalar `y` of the single unit), the morphology `\vect{m}` and the controller `\vect{\theta}` of chapters 2.5 and 3 (bold also separates them from the mass `m` of chapter 4), and in chapter 4 `\vect{q}`, `\vect{v}`, `\vect{c}`, `\vect{\tau}_{\mathrm{act}}`, `\vect{F}_{\mathrm{a}}`, `\vect{f}_{\mathrm{c}}`, `\vect{a}`, `\vect{a}_0`, `\vect{\omega}`, `\vect{w}_i`, `\vect{v}_{\mathrm{cm}}`, `\vect{F}`, `\vect{g}_{\perp}`, `\mat{M}`, `\mat{J}`, `\mat{R}`, `\mat{D}`, `\mat{K}_v`. Accents go outside: `\dot{\vect{v}}`, `\hat{\vect{F}}`. The commented-out PCA section of chapter 2 was not converted. Chapters 5 to 7 must be written with the macros from the start.
- **Sources of the background sections** (supervisor's request, 2026-09-21). Each of 2.1 to 2.4 names its main source in its opening block: 2.1 Sutton and Barto; 2.2 Goodfellow et al. (worded as "a textbook treatment is given by", NOT "follows", because the section was written from course slides: the author may replace it with the source actually used); 2.3 Eiben and Smith, then Hansen's tutorial (CMA-ES) and Deb's book (multi-objective); 2.4 Hebb + Gerstner and Kistler (article and book). Every figure is mentioned in the body text with `\autoref` (checked 2026-09-21: all are; only the two subfigure labels of `fig:drone-actuators` are unreferenced, by design).
- **Formulas and figures only when they add insight** (author's rule, 2026-09-17). A formula that merely restates prose is left out.
- **Cross-chapter pointers: keep to a minimum** (author's rule). Chapter 3 has none. Subsection 2.5.3 has two (to chapters 3 and 5), approved.
- **Bibliography.** `chapters/Bibliografia.bib`, biblatex + biber, `style=numeric, sorting=nyt`. Keep the existing key style `authorYEARkeyword` (e.g. `gupta2021embodied`). Proceedings versions preferred over preprints; arXiv only when nothing else exists (`@misc` with `howpublished = {\url{...}}`).
- **Glossary.** `chapters/XX_Glossario/XX_Glossario.tex` is a manual `itemize` of acronyms in order of first appearance. Add every new acronym there.
- **Figures.** Go in `images/<chapter folder>/` (folders now match chapter names, see §7). Tikz figures use the preamble's shared styles (see the Hebbian locality figure in chapter 2). Figure titles: write "p-value" not "p", "p-value < 0.001" below one per mille, "progress" not "P", bare figure-level titles with no parenthetical details.
- **Reference drone naming.** Plots call it "Bixler (generalist controller)" (`pareto_fronts.BIXLER_LABEL`); chapter 3 prose calls it "the reference drone" / "the reference body". The title page (author's uncommitted edit) now reads "Hebbian Co-Design of Fixed Wing Drones"; prose so far says "winged drone" and "fixed-wing drone" interchangeably, keep "winged drone" as the neutral phrase outside chapter 4.
- **Author-review protocol.** Questions from the author ("don't you think…?", "what do you think about…?") are answered, not acted on; edits wait for an explicit go-ahead. Structural changes are proposed as a brief first.

## 5. Decisions and promises per chapter

### Chapter 2, section 2.5 Related Work (written 2026-09-17)

- Order: 2.5.1 Co-Design of Morphology and Control → 2.5.2 Evolved and Learned Plasticity → 2.5.3 Positioning of This Thesis. (Co-design is the problem, plasticity the tool, then the gap.)
- 2.5.1 opens with the **bilevel co-design formula** `eq:codesign-bilevel` (outer max over body m, inner max over controller θ) and uses it to compare how each work pays for the inner level. Papers: Sims 1994, Lipson & Pollack 2000, Cheney et al. 2018 (premature convergence, morphological innovation protection), Eiben & Hart 2020, Gupta et al. 2021 (DERL: PPO from scratch per body, Baldwin effect, >1000 CPUs), Muff et al. 2026 (hexacopters: (μ+λ)-ES on 36 body parameters, PPO from scratch per body, <1000 bodies), Bergonti et al. 2024 (same lab, NSGA-II outer, trajectory optimization inner, no learned controller), Gupta et al. 2022 MetaMorph (morphology-conditioned universal policy).
- 2.5.2 opens with Floreano & Urzelai 2000 and the **"Born to Learn" lesson** (plasticity is only selected when something varies between or within lifetimes). Miconi et al. 2018 gets the second formula `eq:diffplast` (fixed weight + α·Hebbian trace, all trained by gradient), Backpropamine 2019 one clause. Najarro & Risi 2020 (ABCD + η per synapse, ES, random weights every episode, static net wins on the intact body), Pedersen & Risi 2021, Ferigo et al. 2023, van Diggelen et al. 2025 (one shared ABCD set, random init per robot, CMA-ES, MARL baselines beaten).
- 2.5.3 = the gap paragraph (enumerate of the three ways co-design works pay for the inner level; the plasticity works all start from random weights or co-train by gradient) + **one** paragraph naming the thesis at a high level, mapping its plastic part onto `eq:diffplast`. The longer version with method details and the Raghu/Soltoggio justifications was **discarded on the author's decision**; those justifications go to chapter 5.
- Notes the author asked for are in place: Gupta = RL-trained controller per body; Muff = EA owns the morphology; Bergonti = trajectory optimization, no controller; Miconi = differentiable not evolved; van Diggelen = converges from random init, same rule set for the whole swarm.

### Chapter 3, Challenges (written 2026-09-17; renamed from "Problems" 2026-09-21, folder `03_Challenges`, labels `ch:challenges`, `sec:challenge-*`)

- Opening: setting in one paragraph (winged drone, forest, distance and energy), then the **three sections as three steps of one argument** ending in the requirement "a controller that adapts to the body it is given, at a cost small enough to sit inside the body search".
- 3.1 Sequential design: fix m, solve θ*(m) only; the body bounds energy per metre (airframe) and manoeuvrability (control authority); ends "a body that was never tried cannot be found". **Left out on purpose:** the reference drone + specialist controller as the sequential-design baseline. There is a `% Possible addition` comment in the source; the author may draw it from the standard-mydrone results later.
- 3.2 Morphology optimization: fix θ_G, search m; **`eq:ranking-flip`** (two bodies ranked one way under the shared controller, the other under their own); the generalist undervalues bodies at the edges of its training distribution, which is where better bodies are; training a specialist after the search cannot recover a discarded body; ends with the requirement "a score that reflects what each body can do with a controller adapted to it".
- 3.3 Feasibility: **`tab:feasibility`** (measured + hypothetical costs, see §6); noise multiplier (60 forests per body); the works that train per body stay <1000 bodies or need >1000 CPUs; ends with the shape any answer must have (controller paid once; adaptation to the scored body from elsewhere, at rollout cost) and the empirical question.
- No figure in chapter 3 (equation + table carry it). No cross-chapter references. No method names. No platform details.

### Chapter 4, section 4.1 Genesis: The Simulator (written 2026-09-18)

- Subsection order (author's three headings kept, two added): 4.1.1 **Architecture and Taichi Kernels** (new, placed first because the pros and cons need its vocabulary) → 4.1.2 Advantages and Disadvantages → 4.1.3 Physics Simulation → 4.1.4 Aerodynamics Simulation (run-in `\paragraph` heads: elements and local flow, coefficients, forces and points of application, interaction, propeller, coupling and safeguards, limits of the model) → 4.1.5 **Simulation Noise** (new).
- **No code** anywhere (author's rule for this section): formulas and three tikz figures only (`fig:taichi-kernel` life of a kernel, `fig:sim-step` one control step, `fig:aero-coefficients` C_L/C_D of the reference wing, NACA 3416, AR 3.5, curve data computed from the solver's formulas).
- Author's instructions: stochasticity is described **without CRN** (never mention common random numbers in chapter 4); the aerodynamic noise is presented as a robustness measure (domain randomization, Tobin 2017 / Peng 2018).
- Everything was fact-checked against the vendored `genesis/` (0.3.7 + lab aero solver) and the production configs. Facts that differ from what one would assume: state exchange with PyTorch is a **GPU-to-GPU copy, not zero-copy**; Taichi's Python frontend is from the DiffTaichi paper (both Taichi papers are cited); 0.3.7 runs in `ti.field` mode, so compilation is scene-specific; the integrator is the default `approximate_implicitfast`; constraint solver CG; `enable_collision=False` (trees are tensors because geometry is shared across envs); aero forces are explicit, evaluated at 100 Hz on the start-of-substep state; production aero config is the **inline** `SimpleDroneAeroParameters` (force cap 30 N, fuselage C_D0 0.35), not `aero_parameters.yaml`; wing section parameters come from `src/naca_generation/naca4.csv` (XFOIL), tail = NACA 0216 row.
- **Attribution of the aerodynamic solver (author's instruction, 2026-09-18).** 4.1.4 opens with a credit paragraph: the solver was designed and implemented by **Andrea Vicari** (PhD candidate, also on the title page) as part of his still unpublished doctoral research; it is not a contribution of this thesis, the credit is entirely his, the author thanks him, and 4.1.4 documents the model "as it was used" without being the reference description of his work. No citation exists yet: add one if his work is published before submission. The section intro points to this credit, and the rest of 4.1 says "the solver used in this thesis", never "of this thesis". Keep this wording in chapters 5 to 7 and thank him again in the acknowledgements.
- Deliberately neutral wording, because the code and the textbook differ (see §8): the force rotation is given literally as `F = R_z(β) R_y(α) (D, 0, L)^T`, the slipstream as "corrects the axial component of the flow seen by the elevator through a gain k_s" (no sign), and the per-episode randomization of the aerodynamic *parameters* is **not mentioned** (it never runs). The missing ω × r term is stated openly in "Limits of the model".
- One cross-chapter pointer: `\autoref{tab:feasibility}` in 4.1.2. Two intra-chapter pointers: `sec:flight-environment`, `sec:drone-model` (labels added to the empty sections).
- 4.2 must describe the tensor forests, the depth rays and the geometric collision test (4.1.3 defers them there); 4.3 must describe the six joints, the NACA genes and the airfoil table (4.1.4 defers them there); sensor noise, actuation latency and servo offsets are deferred to "the definition of the task".

### Chapter 4, section 4.2 Flight Environment (written 2026-09-18)

- Intro of 4.2 (before the three subsections): corridor (W = 100 m, start 30 m before the first tree, 15 m altitude, r = 0.75 m, trees infinitely tall so avoidance is planar), forests as F × T tensors, then run-in `\paragraph` heads **Perception** (20 sectors over an 80° fan, 30 m range, nearest trunk surface or lateral wall, fan narrows with cos(roll); it is a *sector* sensor, not a ray caster: a trunk blocks every sector its angular extent overlaps), **Collision** (`eq:forest-collision`, rectangle r × (b/2 cos φ + r), margin 1 cm in evaluation and 20 cm in training), **End of a flight** (defines *progress* = x at the end of the flight; the second objective is only named "energy spent per metre", to be defined later).
- Author's roles for the three generators, used verbatim as the storyline: **uniform** = internal tests, **growing** = every result of the thesis, **Latin hypercube** = explored to remove overlapping trunks and tree walls, then discarded to keep the forests natural.
- **Author's rule (2026-09-18): the difference between inner-loop forests and exam forests is NOT mentioned in chapter 4**; it belongs to the methodology chapter. 4.2 says only "The lengths and densities used in the experiments are given with the method", and mentions the per-forest randomization of λ_0 as "an option used during training" without values. Chapter 5 must therefore give: training course (150 m, λ 0…3 → 4), search course (100 m, 0 → 5), exam course (300 m, 1 → 5.5), forests per evaluation, the 80 m gate.
- Insight formulas kept: linear profile `eq:forest-density`, inverse-CDF sampling `eq:forest-inverse-cdf`, survival of a blind straight flight `eq:forest-survival` / `eq:forest-survival-growing` (exponential, memoryless in a uniform forest; quadratic exponent in a growing one), nearest-neighbour probability 1 − exp(−nπd²) inline.
- "Latin hypercube" is the author's heading and the project's name (`forest_mode: latin`), but the generator is a **jittered / fully stratified sampling (one tree per cell)**, not LHS proper; the text says so explicitly ("The name is used loosely") and cites McKay et al. 1979. The `lattice` mode gets one sentence as an earlier, more regular variant.
- Figures (all top views, trunks to scale, same 300 m × 100 m corridor and about the same number of trees so they compare directly): `forest_sensing`, `forest_uniform` (975 trees), `forest_growing` (λ 1 → 5.5, 975 trees, with a trees-per-10-m strip against the profile), `forest_random_vs_stratified` (one tree per 25 m², overlaps and closed gaps highlighted), `forest_latin` (cells 8 → 3.4 m, 988 trees). Script: `images/04_Simulation_Environment/make_forest_figures.py` (loads `src/winged_drone_train/perception/forest.py` by path, torch on CPU, fixed seeds; rerun it to regenerate). Two-colour palette (#0f8a5f trees, #D55E00 accent) checked for colour-blind separation; highlighted items also differ by shape/outline.

### Chapter 4, section 4.3 Drone Model (written 2026-09-18)

- Subsections: 4.3.1 Drone Shape and Actuators (run-in heads: servomotors and gear ratios, masses, energy), **4.3.2 Sensors (new, author's request: the depth-sensor paragraph and `fig:forest-sensing` were moved here from 4.2 "since it's more related to the drone")**, 4.3.3 Drone Genome (`tab:drone-genome`), 4.3.4 The Standard Drone. 4.2 keeps Collision and End of a flight and points to `subsec:drone-sensors`.
- **Cost of transport is defined here** (`eq:drone-cot`, CoT = E/(m g Δx), nominal mass, Δx = progress) together with the propeller power law `eq:drone-prop-power` and a qualitative servo power model (mechanical power + loss ∝ torque², never negative; no motor/ESC losses). How CoT is averaged over flights (ratio of means per body) is **left to chapter 5**.
- Sensors table `tab:drone-sensors`: 28 measurements (altitude, quaternion, world-frame velocity, 20 depth sectors) with their noise in physical units (0.1 m, 0.01, 0.4 / 0.2 m/s, 1.5 m) + 8 non-measured inputs (last 7 commands, commanded speed) = 36. Stated as absent: position along/across the corridor, angular rates, airspeed, joint angles, any information on the body. The critic's privileged observations are NOT in chapter 4 (chapter 5).
- **`wing_span` gene = span of ONE panel** (tip to tip = 2 b_p + 0.1 m; aerodynamic aspect ratio = (2 b_p + 0.1)/chord = 7.5 for the reference drone, NOT 3.5). This corrected `fig:aero-coefficients` in 4.1.4 (now AR 7.5, α_s 12.0°, C_Lmax 0.98, (L/D)max 18.7) and every wingspan-dependent number in 4.2 (b = 1.4 m in the collision test: blind flight 11 m / 26 m, closed gaps 64 % → 39 %). **The b of 4.2 is the width used by the simulator's collision test, the combined span of the two panels (2 b_p, 1.4 m), not the tip-to-tip wingspan (1.5 m)**; the text says so and states that the test ignores the fuselage, the body length and the sweep. Switching 4.2 to 1.5 m was tried and reverted on 2026-09-21 (see `CH4_CORRECTIONS.md` B3).
- The sweep/twist "multipliers" are presented as **gear ratios** (range 0.7/k and 0.5/k rad, torque 0.75 k and 0.6 k N m, damping ∝ k², friction ∝ k); tail joints ±0.25 rad, 1 N m, no gear.
- Standard drone vs Bixler 3 (author's instruction): "the dimensions and the section of its wing are those of the H-King Bixler 3", cited to the product page (`hobbyking2026bixler3`: 1.55 m span, 0.95 m length, 0.89 kg) and to Bergonti et al. The correspondence of dimensions and wing section is stated on the author's authority (author's decision 2026-09-18: no further check needed).

- **Aerodynamic solver vs textbook (found while fact-checking 4.1, 2026-09-18; author to decide, thesis wording is neutral until then).** (1) `_rot_yz(alpha, beta)` applies `R_y(+α)` while `alpha = atan2(w_z, w_x)` is measured towards +z: drag ends up mirrored about the chord instead of parallel to the relative wind, i.e. the resultant is tilted aft by 2α and the force along the wind is `D cos 2α + L sin 2α` (checked numerically: D = 1, L = 10, α = 8° gives 3.7 instead of 1). The sibling `lisparrow.py` has the correct signs. (2) In the x-aft aero frames `v_body_tail.x -= slip * v_ind` *lowers* the airspeed at the elevator inside the propeller slipstream.
